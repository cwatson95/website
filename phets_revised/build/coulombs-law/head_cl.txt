title=Coulomb&rsquo;s Law &mdash; the inverse-square force between two charges
h1=Coulomb&rsquo;s Law
sub=the force between two charges you can drag, on PhET&rsquo;s macro and atomic scales: equal and opposite, proportional to each charge, attractive or repulsive by the sign of their product, and falling as the square of the distance
---
  ===================================================================
  coulombs-law.html — Coulomb's law between two charges on a track, on
  PhET's macro scale (microcoulombs, centimetres) and atomic scale
  (elementary charges, picometres)
  ===================================================================

  Remake of PhET Interactive Simulations' Coulomb's Law (University of
  Colorado Boulder, phet.colorado.edu, CC-BY 4.0). New code; no PhET code
  or assets.  PhET's build 1.0.32 (docs/phets/coulombs-law_en.html) was
  read only for its model constants, each cited below as "PhET's value".
  It runs on the same inverse-square engine as gravity-force-lab.html
  (build/gravity-force-lab/p2_engine.js and p4_lab.js, shared verbatim).

  Five views.  MACRO SCALE and ATOMIC SCALE are PhET's two screens: two
  charges on a track, dragged along a grid, a force arrow on each (equal
  and opposite, together for unlike charges and apart for like ones) to
  one printed scale, the force in PhET's decimal or scientific notation, a
  draggable ruler and a scale bar.  THE INVERSE SQUARE: |F| against the
  distance on log-log axes (slope -2), on linear axes, and as |F| r^2,
  flat at |k q1 q2|.  THE CHARGE PRODUCT: the force over every pair of
  charges — like signs repel, unlike attract, contours |q1 q2| = const —
  and the force as a straight line through zero in q2 and in q1 q2.
  ELECTRICITY AND GRAVITY: an electron and a proton on log-log axes, the
  two inverse-square laws parallel and 2.27e39 apart at every distance;
  and the equal masses that would pull as hard as the macro charges.

  WHAT IS EXACT
  -------------
  F = k q1 q2 / r^2 with k = 1/(4 pi eps0), eps0 = 8.8541878188e-12 F/m
  (CODATA 2022), so k = 8.987551786e9 N m^2 C^-2; e = 1.602176634e-19 C,
  exact since the 2019 SI; G, m_e, m_p and the Bohr radius, CODATA 2022 —
  all read from scipy.constants 1.18 in build/coulombs-law/derive.py and
  pinned in selfTest to its refvals.txt.  r is the distance between
  centres, exact for rigid uniformly charged spheres and for point charges.

  WHAT IS MODELLED (PhET's choices, reproduced)
  ---------------------------------------------
  PhET's values.  Macro: charges -10..10 uC in whole microcoulombs, spheres
  6.75 mm in radius, centres on a 1-mm grid within +-5 cm, and a 1-mm gap
  asked for between surfaces — which PhET's own arithmetic turns into
  14 mm between centres (6.75 + 6.75 + 1 = 14.4999... mm in floating point,
  snapped down), a 0.5-mm gap; reproduced and pinned.  Atomic: -10..10 e,
  0.95-pm markers on a 1-pm grid within +-50 pm, a 0.1-pm gap.  Defaults:
  -4 uC at -1 cm and +8 uC at +2 cm; -2e at -20 pm and +4e at +20 pm.
  PhET uses k = 8.987551e9 and e = 1.6021766208e-19 (CODATA 2014), so its
  readouts differ from these by 9e-8 (macro) and 1e-7 (atomic).

  NOT MODELLED
  ------------
  PhET's cartoon pullers and pushers, sounds and screen-reader
  descriptions.  Induced charge: two conducting spheres this close would
  redistribute their charge and depart from the point-charge law.  The
  charges' masses: nothing here moves on its own.
