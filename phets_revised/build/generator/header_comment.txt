  ===================================================================
  generator.html — Generator: water turns a bar magnet beside a pickup
  coil, and the changing flux through the coil drives a current —
  Faraday's law, with the flux integrated over the coil's face
  ===================================================================

  Remake of PhET Interactive Simulations' Generator (University of
  Colorado Boulder, phet.colorado.edu, CC-BY 4.0). New code; no PhET
  code or assets. The original is docs/phets/generator_en.html
  (version 1.0.7); its single screen is the first view here. Built on
  the same engine as magnets-and-electromagnets.html and
  magnet-and-compass.html: the shared parts and the Python twin live
  in build/magnets-and-electromagnets/ (e1_core.js, e3_lines.js,
  e5_gen.js, v_common.js, v_screens.js, v_analysis.js, derive.py), and
  this page carries only what it uses — the electromagnet, the bar
  magnet screen and the far-field view are stripped at build time and
  the build asserts that no trace of them is left.

  Six views. THE GENERATOR — PhET's screen: a faucet, a water wheel
  carrying the bar magnet and showing its speed in its hub, a pickup
  coil of 1-4 loops whose area is adjustable, a light bulb or a
  voltmeter, electrons, the field of needles, a compass and a field
  meter. FLUX AND EMF — the flux through the coil over one turn of the
  wheel and the EMF that follows it, against the single cosine a
  distant magnet would give. THE COIL FACE — the two ways of adding
  the field over the coil's disc, strip by strip up its diameter,
  whose areas are the two estimates of the flux: B integrated along
  each chord, against the in-page value PhET carries across it. FIELD LINES
  — the exact field lines of the magnet the wheel carries. ON THE AXIS
  — B and mu0 H along that magnet's axis in closed form. THE NEEDLE —
  the compass as a damped rotor (Gauss's method).

  WHAT IS EXACT
  -------------
  The magnet is a uniformly magnetised cylinder, 250 mm long and 50 mm
  across (PhET's 250 x 50 x 50 bar; PhET's own notes: "modeled in
  MathCAD as a horizontal cylinder"). Its B field is exactly that of
  an ideal finite solenoid carrying the bound current K = M x n round
  its side wall, in closed form everywhere through Bulirsch's
  generalized complete elliptic integral (Derby & Olbert, Am. J. Phys.
  78, 229 (2010)), in 3-D. THIS DIFFERS FROM PhET, which interpolates
  three grids precomputed once in MathCAD; those grids are a sampling
  of this same field (median 2.2e-7 G per G of strength over their
  15,971 points).

  The flux through one loop is the AREA integral of B_x over its disc,
  which stands perpendicular to the page: 16-point Gauss-Legendre in
  radius by 32-point trapezoid in angle, asserted against scipy's
  dblquad to 1e-10 G mm^2 at every angle, and unchanged by doubling
  either rule. One turn is sampled at 128 angles and kept as a Fourier
  series, so Phi(theta) and dPhi/dtheta are exact between the samples;
  only odd cosines survive (Phi is even in theta and reverses every
  half turn), which is asserted, not assumed. The EMF is Faraday's
  law, -N dPhi/dt = -N omega dPhi/dtheta: linear in N and in omega,
  reversed by reversing either, zero where the flux is stationary, and
  checked against a finite difference of Phi itself and against
  -dPhi/dt taken by stepping the page's own clock. Because the flux is
  not a cosine, the EMF does NOT peak with the magnet across the coil:
  it peaks at 24.79 degrees for the smallest coil and 37.95 for the
  largest (python, bounded maximiser), with the value at 90 degrees
  down to 0.29-0.60 of the peak.

  PhET's own flux is a line sample: B_x every 5 mm along the coil's
  vertical diameter in the page, each weighted by its chord, with the
  field off the page taken to be the field in it. That overestimates
  the flux by 13 % for the smallest coil and 57 % for the largest,
  which the coil-face view draws. It is not an error in PhET: its EMF
  is deliberately a relative number against its own full scale, and
  this page's volts are only volts because the integral is done.

  Field lines are exact lines of B in the page (RK4 along B/|B|, the
  side wall crossed exactly), seeded at equal flux. Everything is
  pinned in selfTest to build/magnets-and-electromagnets/derive.py
  (scipy dblquad, quad, brentq, solve_ivp, numpy FFT).

  WHAT IS MODELLED
  ----------------
  The compass is a damped rotor, phi'' = kappa |B| sin(theta_B - phi)
  - gamma phi', with PhET's kinematic-compass constants converted to
  seconds (kappa = 6.25 /(G s^2), gamma = 2 /s); on this screen it
  rides a turning field and lags it. The drag of the coil on the wheel
  is PhET's own phenomenological rule (its documentation: "Drag on the
  turbine caused by the pickup coil"): rpm = flow/100 x (1 - drag) x
  60, with drag a linear ramp in N x area from 0.01 to 0.2 scaled by
  the magnet's strength. A real back-torque would need the circuit's
  resistance, which neither PhET nor this page models. The electrons'
  speed is a picture of the current, not a drift speed. The bulb's
  brightness and the voltmeter's needle are the EMF against PhET's
  full scale, 1.2e5 G mm^2 per frame at 25 frames/s = 0.30 mV here.

  WHAT IS NOT MODELLED
  --------------------
  No circuit: no resistance, no current, no power — the pickup coil is
  an open circuit whose EMF is read, as in PhET. The magnet is bolted
  to the wheel and cannot be dragged. Nothing loads the wheel but the
  drag rule above. PhET's keyboard and sound layers, its projector
  colours and its developer controls are not reproduced. The water
  falls on the LEFT of the wheel here, so the magnet's angle
  increases; PhET steps its own angle the other way, which only
  reverses the sign of the EMF.

  WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM
  ---------------------------
  One measured constant: mu0 = 1.25663706127e-6 N/A^2 (CODATA 2022,
  NIST). PhET's values, read from the 1.0.7 build: the 250 x 50 bar,
  0-300 G at 75%; the pickup coil's 1-4 loops and 20-100% of
  70685 mm^2 at 50%; 60 RPM at full flow, the drag range 0-0.2 and its
  ramp; maxEMF 1.2e5; the 5 mm sample spacing; the 40-px needle grid
  and brightness rule; the compass constants; the 1024 x 618 layout
  and the objects' places. 1 PhET model px = 1 mm on this page (a
  stated scale, not PhET's).
