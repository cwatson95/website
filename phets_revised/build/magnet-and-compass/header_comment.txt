  ===================================================================
  magnet-and-compass.html — Magnet and Compass: a bar magnet, the
  exact field it makes, the compass and the field meter that read it,
  and the physics underneath: field lines, the field on the axis, the
  far-field dipole, and the needle as a pendulum
  ===================================================================

  Remake of PhET Interactive Simulations' Magnet and Compass
  (University of Colorado Boulder, phet.colorado.edu, CC-BY 4.0). New
  code; no PhET code or assets. The original is
  docs/phets/magnet-and-compass_en.html (version 1.0.7); its single
  screen (PhET's Bar Magnet screen) is the first view here. Built on
  the same engine as magnets-and-electromagnets.html and
  generator.html: the shared parts and the Python twin live in
  build/magnets-and-electromagnets/ (e1_core.js, e3_lines.js,
  e4_far.js, v_common.js, v_screens.js, v_analysis.js, derive.py), and
  this page carries only what it uses — the electromagnet is stripped
  at build time and the build asserts that no trace of it is left.

  Five views. THE BAR MAGNET — PhET's screen: drag the magnet, flip
  it, set its strength; a field of needles, a compass, a field meter
  (B, Bx, By, theta in G or T), see inside, the Earth. FIELD LINES —
  the exact field lines in the page, seeded at equal flux, and the
  magnet's bound surface current. ON THE AXIS — B and mu0 H along the
  axis in closed form, against the point dipole. THE FAR FIELD — |B|
  on the axis and the bisector against the dipole, and the approach to
  it as the octupole C/r^2. THE NEEDLE — the compass as a damped
  rotor, its period against |B| (Gauss's method).

  WHAT IS EXACT
  -------------
  The magnet is a uniformly magnetised cylinder, 250 mm long and 50 mm
  across (PhET's 250 x 50 x 50 bar; PhET's own notes: "modeled in
  MathCAD as a horizontal cylinder"). Its B field is exactly that of
  an ideal finite solenoid carrying the bound current K = M x n round
  its side wall, evaluated in closed form everywhere with Bulirsch's
  generalized complete elliptic integral (Derby & Olbert, Am. J. Phys.
  78, 229 (2010)), in 3-D; the page is the plane through the axis.
  THIS DIFFERS FROM PhET, which interpolates three grids precomputed
  once in MathCAD, clamps |B| at the strength, and returns zero beyond
  2.5 m. Those grids are a sampling of this same field: over their
  15,971 points (read from the 1.0.7 build by derive.py) they agree
  with the closed form to a median 2.2e-7 G per G of strength, 97.9%
  within 1e-4, worst 4.6e-3 beside the pole-face rim, where the exact
  field has its logarithmic edge singularity. On the axis the general
  form reduces to the elementary cosine difference and is asserted
  against it. The far field tends to the point dipole of moment M V:
  the limit is taken numerically (Richardson at 2, 4, 8 m) and the
  approach matches the octupole coefficient 2(b^2 - 3R^2/4) in closed
  form. Field lines are exact lines of B in the page (RK4 along B/|B|,
  the magnet's side wall crossed exactly), seeded at equal flux.
  Everything is pinned in selfTest to
  build/magnets-and-electromagnets/derive.py (scipy dblquad over the
  current sheet, quad, brentq, solve_ivp).

  WHAT IS MODELLED
  ----------------
  The compass is a damped rotor, phi'' = kappa |B| sin(theta_B - phi)
  - gamma phi', with PhET's kinematic-compass constants converted to
  seconds: kappa = 0.01 /G per frame^2 and gamma = 0.08 per frame at
  25 frames/s give 6.25 /(G s^2) and 2 /s; a flip kicks it 0.75 rad/s
  (PhET's 0.03 per frame). PhET snaps the needle to the field above
  10 G; this page integrates the rotor at every field, so it rings
  near the magnet as a real needle does. The needle brightness is
  PhET's display rule, min(1, 2(|B|/300 G)^(1/2.7)).

  WHAT IS NOT MODELLED
  --------------------
  The Earth adds no field (as in PhET, it is a picture, and it is
  drawn as a plain globe: PhET's two hemisphere images are not
  reproduced). The magnet feels no forces and nothing moves by itself
  but the compass needle. PhET's keyboard and sound layers, its
  projector colours and its developer controls are not reproduced.

  WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM
  ---------------------------
  One measured constant: mu0 = 1.25663706127e-6 N/A^2 (CODATA 2022,
  NIST). PhET's values, read from the 1.0.7 build: the 250 x 50 bar,
  0-300 G at 75%, the 40-px needle grid and brightness rule, the
  compass constants, the 1024 x 618 layout and the objects' places.
  1 PhET model px = 1 mm on this page (a stated scale, not PhET's).
