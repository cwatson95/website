  ===================================================================
  charges-and-fields.html — Charges and Fields: point charges on a
  plane, their exact electric field and potential, the equipotentials
  and field lines they make, the potential as a landscape, the field
  as a vector sum, and the multipole far field
  ===================================================================

  Remake of PhET Interactive Simulations' Charges and Fields (University
  of Colorado Boulder, phet.colorado.edu, CC-BY 4.0). New code; no PhET
  code or assets. The original is docs/phets/charges-and-fields_en.html
  (version 1.0.67); its single screen is the first view here.

  Five views.  THE PLANE — PhET's screen: drag +1 nC and -1 nC charges
  and E-field sensors out of the bin onto an 8 m x 5 m plane, drop them
  back in the bin to remove them.  The field is drawn on a 0.5 m grid of
  fixed-length arrows whose brightness follows |E| up to 5 V/m ("direction
  only" makes them all bright), the voltage on 0.1 m cells that saturate
  at +-40 V.  The voltage sensor reads V at its crosshair and its "plot"
  button traces the equipotential through it; the E-field sensors read
  |E| and its direction; there is a tape measure, a grid and value
  labels.  FIELD LINES — the same charges, lines traced along E from the
  charges of the majority sign, an evenly spaced family of equipotentials
  crossing them at right angles, and the null points where E = 0 found and
  classified.  THE LANDSCAPE — V(x, y) as a height surface (clipped, and
  the clip printed): peaks at the positive charges, wells at the negative
  ones, the field everywhere pointing straight downhill, and the plotted
  equipotentials drawn as the level cuts they are.  SUPERPOSITION — at a
  probe, each charge's field drawn head to tail and the chain closing on
  the total; the potential as a signed sum of the charges' terms.  THE FAR
  FIELD — the potential along a ray against distance on log axes, the
  exact sum against its leading multipole term, and r^(l+1) V(r) taken to
  its limit numerically.

  WHAT IS EXACT
  -------------
  V(r) = k sum q_i / |r - r_i| and E = k sum q_i (r - r_i) / |r - r_i|^3:
  the 3-D Coulomb field of point charges held in the plane z = 0 (PhET's
  model: "the three dimensional Maxwell's equations are used, the charges
  are confined to move along a two dimensional surface"), in closed form
  at every point drawn.  The equipotentials are traced along z x E by RK4
  and projected back onto V = V0 by Newton after every step, so every
  vertex sits on the level set to 1e-12; they close on themselves or end
  at the edge of the traced region.  Field lines are RK4 on E/|E| with the
  step set by the local length scale min(distance to a charge, |E|/|grad E|).
  Null points are Newton roots of E = 0 with the analytic Jacobian.  The
  multipole coefficients along a ray come from the Legendre expansion of
  1/|r - d|, exact to all orders beyond the farthest charge and checked
  against the tensor contraction for the monopole, dipole and quadrupole.
  The tape reads V(A) - V(B), which the suite checks against the line
  integral of E along it.  Everything is pinned in selfTest to
  build/charges-and-fields/derive.py (scipy brentq on rays for the
  equipotential loops, solve_ivp for the field lines, fsolve for the
  nulls, quad for the tape).

  WHAT IS MEASURED
  ----------------
  One constant: eps0 = 8.8541878188e-12 F/m (CODATA 2022, from the NIST
  constants page, stored verbatim), so k = 1/(4 pi eps0) = 8.98755 V m/nC.
  The charges are +-1 nC exactly, as in PhET.

  WHAT IS CHOSEN (PhET's values, read from the 1.0.67 build)
  ----------------------------------------------------------
  The 8 m x 5 m plane and the bin at x in [-1.25, 1.25] m, y in [-2.3,
  -1.7] m; the arrow grid at 0.5 m cell centres, 40 px arrows at 128 px/m
  (0.3125 m); the voltage cells of 0.1 m; the colour saturations at +-40 V
  and 5 V/m; the sensor arrow at 15 px per V/m at 128 px/m (0.1172 m per
  V/m); the charge radius of 12 px (0.094 m); no equipotential seeded
  within 3 cm of a charge; plotted equipotentials cleared whenever a charge
  moves.  PhET's own Coulomb constant is K_CONSTANT = 9 V m/nC, a rounding
  of k, so PhET reads 0.14 % higher than this page (9.00 V at 1 m from
  +1 nC against 8.99 V here).

  WHAT IS NOT MODELLED
  --------------------
  The charges feel no forces and do not move by themselves (this is
  electrostatics, not dynamics); there are no conductors or dielectrics;
  the charges are points (nothing is drawn inside a charge's disc, where
  the field grows without bound).  PhET's projector colour profile and its
  touch and keyboard accessibility layer are not reproduced.
