  magnets-and-electromagnets.html — Magnets and Electromagnets: a bar
  magnet and a coil, the field each makes, the compass and the field
  meter that read it, and the physics underneath: field lines, the
  field on the axis, the far-field dipole, and the needle as a pendulum
  ===================================================================

  Remake of PhET Interactive Simulations' Magnets and Electromagnets
  (University of Colorado Boulder, phet.colorado.edu, CC-BY 4.0). New
  code; no PhET code or assets. The original is
  docs/phets/magnets-and-electromagnets_en.html (version 1.0.7); its two
  screens are the first two views here. Family: magnet-and-compass.html
  (the Bar Magnet screen alone) and generator.html share this engine
  (build/magnets-and-electromagnets/e*.js).

  Six views. THE BAR MAGNET — PhET's screen: drag the magnet, flip it,
  set its strength; a field of needles, a compass, a field meter
  (B, Bx, By, theta in G or T), see inside, the Earth. THE
  ELECTROMAGNET — PhET's screen: a coil of 1-4 loops on a battery
  (-10 to +10 V) or an AC supply (maximum voltage and frequency),
  electrons, needles, compass, meter. FIELD LINES — the exact field
  lines of either source in the page, seeded at equal flux, and the
  magnet's bound surface current. ON THE AXIS — B and mu0 H along the
  magnet's axis in closed form; the coil's loop sum against the
  continuous solenoid. THE FAR FIELD — |B| on the axis and bisector
  against the point dipole, the approach to it as the octupole C/r^2.
  THE NEEDLE — the compass as a damped rotor, its period against |B|.

  WHAT IS EXACT
  -------------
  The bar magnet is a uniformly magnetised cylinder, 250 mm long and
  50 mm across (PhET's 250 x 50 x 50 bar; PhET's own notes: "modeled in
  MathCAD as a horizontal cylinder"). Its B field is exactly that of an
  ideal finite solenoid carrying the bound current K = M x n round its
  side wall, evaluated in closed form everywhere with Bulirsch's
  generalized complete elliptic integral (Derby & Olbert, Am. J. Phys.
  78, 229 (2010)), in 3-D; the page is the plane through the axis.
  THIS DIFFERS FROM PhET, which interpolates three grids precomputed
  once in MathCAD, clamps |B| at the strength, and returns zero beyond
  2.5 m. The grids are a sampling of this same field: over their 15,971
  points (read from the 1.0.7 build by derive.py) they agree with the
  closed form to a median 2.2e-7 G per G of strength, 97.9% within 1e-4,
  worst 4.6e-3 beside the pole-face rim, where the exact field has its
  logarithmic edge singularity. Each coil loop is a thin circular
  filament; its field is Biot-Savart in complete elliptic integrals,
  exact everywhere (B_rho and the flux function are written as single
  cel integrals, because the textbook K,E forms cancel near the axis:
  11% wrong at rho = 1e-6 a). On the axis both reduce to the closed
  forms and are asserted against them; the N-loop coil tends to the
  continuous solenoid as the midpoint rule, O(pitch^2). The far field
  tends to the point dipole of moment M V (magnet) or N I pi a^2 (coil):
  the limit is taken numerically (Richardson at 2, 4, 8 m) and the
  approach matches the octupole coefficients in closed form. Field
  lines are exact lines of B in the page (RK4 along B/|B|, the magnet's
  wall crossed exactly), seeded at equal flux. Everything is pinned in
  selfTest to build/magnets-and-electromagnets/derive.py (scipy dblquad
  over the current sheet, quad, brentq, solve_ivp).

  WHAT IS MODELLED
  ----------------
  The compass is a damped rotor, phi'' = kappa |B| sin(theta_B - phi) -
  gamma phi', with PhET's kinematic-compass constants converted to
  seconds: kappa = 0.01 /G per frame^2 and gamma = 0.08 per frame at
  25 frames/s give 6.25 /(G s^2) and 2 /s; a flip kicks it 0.75 rad/s
  (PhET's 0.03 per frame). PhET snaps the needle to the field above
  10 G, uses a 45-degree step on the Electromagnet screen and snaps
  instantly on the Generator screen; this page integrates the one rotor
  everywhere. The coil current: each supply drives the coil in
  proportion to its own full scale (PhET's normalised current), I =
  I_max V/10 V for the battery and I_max V/110 V for the AC supply,
  with I_max = 704.02 A chosen so that 4 loops at full current make
  PhET's maximum, 300 G, at the centre (computed in code, asserted
  against derive.py). The exact coil then gives 88.5, 170.4, 241.3 and
  300 G at the centre for 1-4 loops where PhET's rule, B proportional
  to N V, gives 75, 150, 225 and 300. The AC frequency is PhET's, 1.25 Hz
  at 100%; quasi-static (the field follows the current instantly).
  Electrons move one loop per second at full current (a picture of the
  current's size and sense, not its drift speed). The needle
  brightness is PhET's display rule, min(1, 2(|B|/300 G)^(1/2.7)).

  WHAT IS NOT MODELLED
  --------------------
  The Earth adds no field (as in PhET, it is a picture). The magnets
  feel no forces and nothing moves by itself but the compass and the
  current. Self-inductance, resistance heating and the coil's own
  magnetisation are ignored; the coil has no iron core. PhET's
  keyboard and sound layers, its projector colours, its Earth
  hemisphere images and its developer controls are not reproduced.

  WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM
  ---------------------------
  One measured constant: mu0 = 1.25663706127e-6 N/A^2 (CODATA 2022,
  NIST). PhET's values, read from the 1.0.7 build: the 250 x 50 bar,
  0-300 G at 75%, the 40-px needle grid and brightness rule, the
  compass constants, the coil (radius 50, wire width 16, loops 1-4),
  the +-10 V battery and 0-110 V AC supply, the 1024 x 618 layout and
  object positions. 1 PhET model px = 1 mm on this page (a stated
  scale, not PhET's).
