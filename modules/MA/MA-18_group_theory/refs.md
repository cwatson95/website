## Finite group theory — honest note
**Elementary finite-group theory (cyclic/dihedral/symmetric groups, Cayley
tables, Lagrange's theorem) is NOT in a text-extractable book on the
`Mathematics/` shelf.** Verified by full-text search: *Schaum's Mathematical
Handbook* has no group-theory section (only statistical "Grouped Data"); Boas has
none; Butkov's group-theory chapter exists but the PDF is an **image scan with no
text layer**, so its pages cannot be pinned. *Introducing Category Theory* uses the
"category of groups" as a warm-up but does not develop elementary group theory as
such. The finite-group code is therefore presented on its own footing (the axioms
are checked directly); no page is cited rather than guess one. Standard texts:
Dummit & Foote, or Artin *Algebra* — not on this shelf.

## Notes (verified)
- Warner's offset (+9) was cross-checked against the back-index entry "exterior
  derivative, 65" and the "Exponential Map / 103" running head.
- Warner is a graduate manifolds text: it gives the **rigorous** Lie-group
  definition (§3, p.82) and the algebra as left-invariant vector fields (p.85). The
  module uses the explicit so(3)/su(2) matrix generators, which are the concrete
  face of that abstract algebra; the abstract construction is the Warner citation.
- The exponential map (§3.29, p.102) is what turns the commutator algebra back into
  finite group elements — exp(θ L) is the rotation generated by L.
