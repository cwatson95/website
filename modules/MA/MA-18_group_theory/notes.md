# MA-18 — Group Theory & Symmetry (notes)

Citation key (full details + PDF pages in `refs.md`): **W** = Warner,
*Foundations of Differentiable Manifolds and Lie Groups*. Pages are the *printed*
book pages. Elementary finite-group theory is not in a text-extractable book on
this shelf (see `refs.md`); the **Lie-group** material is Warner Ch.3.

## 1. What a group is
A **group** (G, ·) is a set with an associative operation, an identity e, and an
inverse for every element. Physics cares because a group is the set of
**symmetries** of something — operations that leave it unchanged. The whole
structure is captured by the **Cayley table** (the multiplication table); the code
verifies the four axioms directly from it (`FiniteGroup.is_group`). A group is
**abelian** if ab=ba.

## 2. The standard finite families
- **Cyclic Zₙ** = integers mod n under addition: abelian, generated by one element.
- **Symmetric Sₙ** = all permutations of n objects under composition, order n!.
  S₃ (order 6) is the **smallest non-abelian group**.
- **Dihedral Dₙ** = the 2n symmetries of a regular n-gon (n rotations + n
  reflections). Realized as permutations of the vertices, **D₃ = S₃** exactly,
  while D₄ (order 8) is a *proper* subgroup of S₄ (order 24).
Code: `cyclic_group`, `symmetric_group`, `dihedral_group`; the test confirms orders,
(non)abelianness, and D₃=S₃.

## 3. Lagrange's theorem
The **order of any subgroup divides the order of the group** — in particular the
order of every element (the size of the cyclic subgroup it generates) divides |G|.
So a group of prime order p is forced to be cyclic Z_p. Code: `element_order`,
`subgroup_orders`; the test checks divisibility for Z₁₂, S₃, S₄, D₅.

## 4. Representations
A **representation** is a homomorphism ρ: G → matrices, ρ(ab)=ρ(a)ρ(b) — it lets
you *compute* with a group using linear algebra (`~MA-04`). Example: Zₙ → SO(2) by
ρ(k) = rotation by 2πk/n; the rotations multiply exactly as the group adds. This is
why the symmetry group of a molecule or crystal predicts its spectra: the
irreducible representations label the states. Code: `cyclic_rep`, `rotation_matrix`.

## 5. Lie groups and Lie algebras
A **Lie group** is a group that is also a smooth manifold (`~MA-17`) — a *continuous*
symmetry [W §3 *Lie Groups* p.82]. Its infinitesimal structure is its **Lie
algebra**: the tangent space at the identity, closed under the commutator bracket
[W *Lie algebra of a Lie group* p.85]. Two run physics:
- **so(3)** — rotations in 3-D. The generators satisfy
  $$[L_i,L_j]=\varepsilon_{ijk}L_k.$$
  The physics convention J = −iL makes them Hermitian with [J_i,J_j]=iε_{ijk}J_k —
  the **angular-momentum** algebra (`~QM-10`). Code: `so3_generators`, `commutator`.
- **su(2)** — the Pauli matrices, with σ_i²=I and
  $$[\sigma_i,\sigma_j]=2i\,\varepsilon_{ijk}\sigma_k.$$
  su(2) is the **double cover** of so(3): the algebra of **spin-½** (`~QM-11`).
  Code: `pauli`. The **exponential map** exp(θ·generator) recovers the group
  element from the algebra [W *Exponential Map* §3.29 p.102] — a finite rotation
  from its generator.

## Where this goes
- `~CM-18`: **Noether's theorem** — every continuous symmetry (a one-parameter Lie
  group) gives a conserved quantity; rotation→angular momentum, translation→momentum.
- `~QM-10`/`~QM-11`: angular momentum and spin *are* the representations of so(3)/su(2);
  the ladder operators are the algebra at work.
- `~QF-03`: gauge theories are built on Lie groups (U(1), SU(2), SU(3)); the gauge
  field is an algebra-valued connection (`~MA-17`).
